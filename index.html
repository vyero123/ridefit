The part that takes weeks

Knowing that 2,300 rpm is a reasonable moment to change up takes a minute to learn. Coordinating the clutch and the throttle takes weeks — and that is what nearly all of this is about: finding the friction zone, handing off between the two pedals, and letting the clutch back out in gear without the car juddering or stalling.

If you ride, the revs and the blip already make sense to you. What does not transfer is that a car's clutch is dry: no oil, no cooling, and far less tolerance of being slipped. Drills 1–5 and the reference section are about exactly that difference.

Everything runs at 0.4× real time to begin with, so the timing is catchable while you learn it. There is a speed control in each drill — wind it up towards 1× as it stops needing to be slow. A real car gives you no such option, which is rather the point.

One invented, clearly stated car. Real pedals, bite points and shift points vary enormously, so everything here is a range and a principle, not your car's numbers. For use at home — never while driving.
